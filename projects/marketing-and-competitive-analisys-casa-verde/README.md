# Casa Verde Competitive Intelligence

An evidence-led competitive intelligence project for Casa Verde Studio, a
sustainable residential interiors studio in Austin, Texas.

## Project Overview

Casa Verde is led by Elena Reyes and operates from East 6th Street in Austin.
The studio has a small team of four and a growing waitlist. Before beginning
positioning work with the team, Elena needed a grounded view of the Austin
sustainable residential design landscape.

This project studies how selected Austin and nearby Hill Country firms present
their residential work, material choices, sustainability claims, service
scope, and public evidence. The work is organized to support internal
decision-making while also producing client-facing and shareable materials.

## Business Problem

Casa Verde needed to understand its competitive landscape before deciding how
to communicate its position. A visual review of design firms would not be
enough: natural-looking imagery does not establish material provenance,
retention, repairability, or environmental performance.

The research therefore focused on comparable residential interior design firms
serving Austin or the nearby Hill Country, with particular attention to:

- residential scope and project types;
- reclaimed, retained, vintage, local, and other material practices;
- sustainability and material-health claims;
- public evidence supporting those claims;
- geographic relevance and documented project locations;
- pricing transparency and unresolved budget fit;
- content quality and useful information for prospective clients.

## Objectives

The project was designed to produce three main outcomes for Elena and the
Casa Verde team:

1. An **internal positioning deck** for team discussion.
2. A **client-facing white paper** about the Austin sustainable design
   landscape.
3. A **shareable infographic** that communicates the main findings in a
   concise visual format.

The supporting research outputs make the evidence and limitations behind those
three outcomes available for review.

## Research Approach

The research used a structured framework covering ten dimensions of each firm:
positioning, project types, signature materials, pricing range, geographic
focus, sustainability claims, ideal client, social presence, content quality,
and weakness or gap.

The selection process prioritized primary sources such as firm websites,
service pages, project pages, Instagram profiles, and relevant corroborating
coverage. Findings distinguish observed content, firm claims, research
inferences, and unknowns. Missing public information is retained as unknown
rather than replaced with an assumption.

The final research covered five firms: Urbane Design, Stelly Selway,
Urbanspace Design Studio, J.Fisher Interiors, and Studio Ferme. Comparable
pricing remained unknown for all five firms, and some Instagram evidence was
limited by signup walls or incomplete access.

## Evidence Limits

This project is a structured review of public evidence, not an environmental
certification or a ranking of service quality. The five firms were selected
for relevance to the research question, so the findings do not represent the
entire Austin market.

The research also preserves several important limits:

- pricing was not verified for the five firms;
- Instagram access was incomplete for some firms;
- public claims are not treated as independent proof of outcomes;
- a limited project sample cannot establish firm-wide practice;
- a positioning map summarizes available evidence, not environmental
  performance.
