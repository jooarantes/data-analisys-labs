# Competitor Discovery

This framework defines what prompt 2 will investigate and what prompts 3 and 4 may carry forward. It contains research criteria, not findings about any firm. It expands all ten dimensions in `docs/templates/competitor_research_schema.md` using Casa Verde's brand bible and brand skill.

## Who counts

The core set is sustainable residential interior design firms serving Austin or nearby Hill Country whose services could plausibly compete for Casa Verde's stated USD $40,000 to $300,000 total project budget. Comparable work matters more than resemblance in photographs.

| Criterion | Evidence to seek | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Residential interior design | Current service page and a named residential project showing interior design involvement, such as room furnishing, renovation, or whole-home work. | Require residential design capability. Mixed-use firms can qualify; separate their residential offer. Exclude builders, landscape-only firms, shops, and architecture-only practices without a documented interior design service. |
| Sustainable practice | A stated commitment to sustainability, reuse, durability, healthy materials, or responsible sourcing, plus a concrete practice or project detail. | Require more than a broad environmental label. Keep firms with only broad claims in a provisional candidate list until evidence can be checked. |
| Honest materials | Explicit descriptions of reclaimed or retained elements, material composition, finish, provenance, repair, or craft. | Seek at least one concrete material or sourcing example. Photographs of timber, stone, or plants cannot establish origin, emissions, species, or environmental performance. |
| Comparable budget | Published total budgets, project minimums, scope-based ranges, or a clearly defined residential service overlapping USD $40,000 to $300,000. | Confirm fit only with comparable price evidence. Unknown prices may justify provisional inclusion when other criteria pass, but label budget fit unverified. Exclude a documented offering wholly outside the band from the core set. |
| Austin or nearby Hill Country | A stated service area naming Austin or nearby Hill Country, supported where possible by a located residential project. | Austin office location alone is insufficient. Look for Austin neighborhoods or nearby communities such as West Lake Hills, Bee Cave, Lakeway, Dripping Springs, and Wimberley. Other Hill Country locations require documented Austin-area service relevance, not a vague Texas-wide claim. |

This is a competitive choice set, not a directory of every firm using natural materials. A smaller verified set is more useful than five weak matches. Do not relax requirements silently to reach the target of five.

## Discovery procedure and shortlist

Search combinations of location, residential interior design, and concrete practices: reclaimed materials, salvage, retained finishes, low-VOC specifications, vintage furniture, local sourcing, repair, and material provenance. These are search leads, not qualification evidence. Search results, directories, and editorial roundups help discover firms; open primary sources before accepting their claims.

For each candidate, inspect services, about, portfolio, and any sustainability or process page. Check the linked Instagram account and use editorial coverage to corroborate project details where useful. Expand the pool until five defensible candidates emerge or document why the target cannot be met. Do not perform client outreach or bypass login walls.

Record name, website, verified Instagram handle or `unknown`, residential evidence, sustainability evidence, honest-material evidence, service geography, budget status, and a one-line inclusion rationale with links. Assign one of these statuses:

- **Confirmed fit:** all criteria supported, including comparable budget evidence.
- **Provisional fit:** strong service, materials, and geographic relevance, with a specific unresolved criterion such as undisclosed pricing. Name the uncertainty in the shortlist and subsequent comparisons.
- **Rejected:** evidence shows a criterion fails. State which criterion and cite the evidence.
- **Unresolved:** insufficient accessible information to assess relevance. Do not confuse this with rejection.

Keep a rejected and unresolved candidate log. Surface the shortlist at prompt 2's checkpoint before detailed browsing and capture. Show confirmed and provisional counts separately.

## Evidence contract for every field

For each field, record: finding; status (`observed`, `firm claim`, `inference`, or `unknown`); source URL and page or post title; access date; publication date if available; screenshot path if captured; and the limitation that affects interpretation. Cite at the field level, not only in a profile bibliography.

Start with the firm's website and Instagram. Use named supplier records, certification registries, or independent project coverage for corroboration when a claim warrants it. Prefer recent, specific evidence about the service being compared. Search snippets do not settle facts when the underlying page is unavailable.

Use `unknown` plus the source attempted and reason for inaccessible or undisclosed facts. Keep contradictory figures with their dates and price bases. A source lacking a price cannot support an estimated price. A blocked social page cannot support a posting-rate estimate.

Maintain original evidence through links and screenshots. Authored summaries must follow the Casa Verde skill. If source wording violates its vocabulary or punctuation rules, paraphrase faithfully, label it as a paraphrase, and preserve the source link. Do not mislabel a paraphrase as a quotation.

## 1. Positioning sentence

**Research question:** What does the firm itself promise, to whom, and through what stated approach?

- Read the homepage headline, about page, residential services copy, and Instagram bio. Record the primary statement and any meaningful channel mismatch.
- Look for an explicit audience, residential service scope, material or sourcing philosophy, and geographic promise. Separate a stylistic description from a claim about environmental practice.
- Write one sentence faithfully representing the firm's self-description. Use a short direct quote only when compliant with brand rules and source quotation limits; otherwise label it `paraphrase of firm positioning`.
- Do not insert sustainability into a firm's positioning merely because photographs contain plants or wood. If no clear statement is available, write `unknown` and report which pages were checked.

**Field output:** one positioning sentence, its source and status, plus a short note on differences between website and Instagram. Keep our interpretation elsewhere.

## 2. Project types served

**Research question:** Which residential decisions and project stages can a client actually hire this firm to handle?

- Distinguish single-room furnishing, kitchens and baths, whole-home renovation, new-build interiors, historic-home work, and vacation homes. Record commercial categories separately.
- Separate services advertised from completed work shown. Identify consultation, furnishing procurement, interior specifications, construction coordination, and styling only where disclosed.
- Review up to five most recent distinct residential projects, or all if fewer are available. Note project dates when published and the sample size. Avoid counting multiple posts of one room as separate projects.
- Record retention and adaptive reuse when documented. A portfolio full of newly fitted rooms does not establish whether original fabric was removed or retained.

**Field output:** advertised scope; demonstrated scope with named examples; sample size and limits. State whether scope is a credible substitute for Casa Verde's residential offer.

## 3. Signature materials

**Research question:** Are recurring materials traceable, and does the firm explain how they will live, age, and be maintained?

- Capture source-named wood species and origins, stone, plaster, textiles, paint systems, furniture, and plants. Separate reclaimed, salvaged, retained, vintage, recycled-content, and newly produced materials.
- Look for named suppliers or craftspeople, geographic origin, prior use, finish chemistry, repairability, and care guidance. Material origin and supplier address are separate facts.
- Call a material recurring only when documented in at least two distinct projects in the sample. Otherwise label it a single-project example or stated preference.
- Do not identify species, solid versus veneer construction, reclaimed status, or certifications from photographs. Do not equate natural materials with low environmental impact.
- For native-plant claims, record named species and claimed regional suitability. Indoor greenery alone is neither proof of native planting nor a meaningful substitute for sourcing evidence.

**Field output:** material, exact source terminology or faithful paraphrase, project examples, provenance evidence, and whether recurrence is documented.

## 4. Pricing range

**Research question:** Can a client compare the firm's cost and service scope with Casa Verde's stated USD $40,000 to $300,000 project band?

- Check services, investment or FAQ pages, booking information, published project descriptions, and accessible social highlights or posts.
- Record total project budget, design fee, hourly rate, retainer, procurement charges, and minimum engagement separately. Include USD, publication date, scope, inclusions, exclusions, and whether a value is a minimum or a range.
- Never compare a design fee directly with a total project budget. Do not multiply hourly rates by invented hours or infer budgets from photography, neighborhoods, press coverage, or home size.
- Identify the smallest and largest documented project scopes in the reviewed portfolio. Use area or room count if published. Without a stated cost, these examples do not become budget endpoints.
- If no comparable price is disclosed, write `unknown`, cite the sources attempted, and leave budget fit unverified. A project that overlaps only part of the band needs a scope note.

**Field output:** separate price components and their bases; smallest and largest observed scopes; confirmed, partial, unverified, or outside-band budget fit.

## 5. Geographic focus in Austin

**Research question:** Where does the firm demonstrate residential experience, rather than merely claim availability?

- Distinguish office location, advertised service area, and documented project locations.
- Capture named neighborhoods and communities such as Hyde Park, East Austin, Tarrytown, West Lake Hills, and nearby Hill Country where a source explicitly identifies them.
- Record project counts by location within the reviewed sample. Label a one-project presence as such rather than calling it geographic dominance.
- Look for local craft relationships, sourcing networks, and climate-responsive choices when documented. A local installation does not prove local manufacture.
- Never infer an exact address, neighborhood, client wealth, or travel policy from a photograph.

**Field output:** stated territory, demonstrated locations with project links, sample concentration, and any limits on local relevance.

## 6. Sustainability claims

**Research question:** What is claimed, what practice is documented, and what can be independently checked?

- **Retention and reuse:** preserved cabinetry or flooring, salvaged materials, vintage furniture, repair, reupholstery, or documented waste diversion. Seek quantities and the scope of any calculation when available.
- **Material health:** named low-VOC finishes, emissions testing, product documentation, and stated standards. Record whether a claim concerns a single product, a specification policy, or a whole project. Do not turn it into a health-outcome claim.
- **Provenance and sourcing:** supplier names, material origin, local production, chain-of-custody information, and a verifiable certification with its subject and validity. A professional credential does not certify every project or material.
- **Durability and care:** repairable construction, replaceable parts, maintenance instructions, reuse planning, and evidence of long service life.
- **Plants and site fit:** explicit species, native range, intended setting, and care or water considerations where relevant. Decorative plants are weak evidence by themselves.

Classify each claim independently: `broad assertion`, `specific practice stated`, `project-linked documentation`, or `independently corroborated`. These describe the strength of public evidence, not a sustainability certification or environmental performance ranking. A firm can have claims at several levels.

**Field output:** claim, subject and scope, evidence level, source, and unresolved verification. Give documented retention and provenance more weight than aesthetic language. Missing documentation remains unknown, not proof that a practice is absent.

## 7. Ideal client persona

**Research question:** Which client's needs, constraints, and working preferences fit the documented offer?

- Prioritize renovation situation, desired service involvement, tolerance for sourcing lead times, preservation goals, material-care preferences, and geographic needs.
- Ground the persona in at least two signals where available, such as a service description and a named project or testimonial. One signal permits only a tentative inference.
- Prefer a needs-based description, such as homeowners retaining original features who want help sourcing repairable furnishings, when evidence supports it.
- Do not invent age, profession, income, family status, or budget from neighborhood or imagery. Use demographic details only if explicitly supported and relevant.

**Field output:** one or two sentences labeled `inference`, supporting links, and confidence with a reason. If the evidence cannot distinguish an audience, write `unknown` rather than a vivid invented persona.

## 8. Social presence cadence

**Research question:** Does Instagram consistently demonstrate the work and help clients understand material choices?

- Use the same trailing 90-day window ending on the research date for every firm. Record exact start and end dates. Count unique visible feed posts, carousels, and reels published inside it; count a carousel once and date pinned posts by publication.
- Report posts per week as observed posts divided by the window length in days, multiplied by seven. Calculate only when the whole window can be inspected. Report the latest visible post date and longest observed gap when possible.
- Classify each post by its primary subject: completed project, process, material or sourcing, care or reuse, people or lifestyle, or service information. Show counts and denominator.
- Note production choices that affect usefulness, such as legible details, sequencing, informative captions, and video explanations. Keep aesthetic taste separate from information value.
- Stories and inaccessible archives are outside the feed count. Do not infer reach, leads, or conversion from public posting or follower counts.
- If access is partial, report the visible sample and limitation. If a login wall prevents inspection, cadence is `unknown`. Zero is valid only for a fully inspected window with no posts.

**Field output:** window, access completeness, count and rate when defensible, content mix, latest visible date, and production notes. Reward useful consistency rather than volume alone.

## 9. Content quality

**Research question:** Does the content make material decisions understandable and help a prospective client assess fit?

Use the same up-to-five-project website sample as above and the most recent 12 accessible Instagram posts within the cadence window, or all if fewer. State the sample and source dates. Evaluate each criterion as `clear`, `partial`, `absent in reviewed sample`, or `unknown due to access`:

- **Project explanation:** brief, constraints, service scope, decisions, and outcomes are understandable.
- **Material specificity:** captions explain origin, composition, retention, care, or a sourcing trade-off rather than only naming a look.
- **Visual usefulness:** overall-room views and details help explain the work; images are legible and accurately associated with a project.
- **Client clarity:** services, process, geographic coverage, and next steps are easy to find. Price nondisclosure belongs in the pricing field and is not automatically poor content.
- **Editorial discipline:** project credits, consistent claims across channels, working links, readable text, and useful captions. Record visible accessibility features only when inspected.

An undated page is not necessarily stale. Support freshness concerns with dated evidence or visibly broken information. Distinguish photography polish from decision-making value.

**Field output:** criterion-level findings and two concise conclusions: what helps a client choose and what information is missing. Cite concrete examples instead of an unsupported overall score.

## 10. Weakness or gap

**Research question:** What supported limitation in the public offer or communication creates a credible opening for Casa Verde?

- Build one short paragraph in this order: observed gap, source and sample limit, consequence for a prospective client, and a possible Casa Verde response.
- Prefer actionable gaps such as provenance claims without project examples, unclear residential scope, or strong material explanations that omit care and repair information.
- Label whether the gap concerns communication, evidence, service scope, or an unresolved question. Public evidence usually cannot establish operational quality.
- Treat a narrow specialty as a strategic choice unless it demonstrably conflicts with the firm's promise. Do not frame unknown prices, inaccessible social content, or fewer posts as automatic weaknesses.
- Include counterevidence when present. If no defensible gap emerges, write `no supported gap identified in reviewed sources` rather than inventing one.

**Field output:** one evidence-led paragraph, confidence and limitation, and a Casa Verde action hypothesis. Any response that depends on Casa Verde having an unverified capability must be conditional.

## Comparison and positioning map for prompt 2

Build the comparison table with all ten fields in schema order. Preserve unknowns, inclusion status, and citations. Do not turn missing evidence into a zero or rank firms by the number of accessible pages.

Use these default map axes because they connect to an actual client choice:

- **Horizontal: public material and sustainability evidence depth.** Use categorical positions: broad assertion, specific practice, recurring project-linked documentation, independently corroborated documentation. Support a firm's position with at least two relevant examples where available; do not let one verified product imply verification of the whole practice. Mark mixed evidence and uncertainty.
- **Vertical: demonstrated residential engagement scope.** Use categorical positions: room furnishing, multi-room interiors, whole-home interiors, interiors integrated with major renovation or new construction. Show a range when the portfolio spans categories. Broader scope is not inherently better or more sustainable.

Treat the map as an evidence summary, not a numeric environmental score. Firms with insufficient evidence belong in an `unknown` note beside the map rather than an invented coordinate. Document any axis revision and why the collected evidence makes it more decision-useful. Keep every profile field even if the map uses only two.

Casa Verde's brand bible supplies its stated identity and budget range. Do not place it above researched firms on evidence depth without comparable project documentation. Label any proposed position as a target to substantiate. An empty region in a five-firm map is a positioning hypothesis, not proof of unmet demand.

## Handoff to prompts 3 and 4

Carry the research date, firm selection limits, source links, and uncertainty into the deck, white paper, and infographic. Separate observed landscape patterns from recommendations and future outlook. Five purposively selected firms cannot establish market share or a citywide trend.

Before handing off, verify that each profile contains all ten fields, each factual finding has a source, all prices retain their basis, every provisional firm remains visibly provisional, screenshots have traceable URLs, and all authored text meets the Casa Verde skill. Summarize the most useful findings without stripping away limitations that change their meaning.
